# Changelog — HyperWorker

## 6.1.0 (2026-08-15)

**The authority layer.** The harness told an agent five things it must never do and said nothing about what follows from that: everything else. Agents filled the silence with the most cautious model they could construct under pressure, and the shape of that model is always the same — route it upward. The result is a decision queue pointed at the one party with the least time and, frequently, the least ability to assess the calls. This release names the line.

The organizing idea is that **authority gates on outcome, not on action size**. The engagement declares the states it has no way back from; anything that cannot reach one of them is a task with a cost, and expensive is not fatal. Two actions of identical size land in different classes because one comes back on its own and the other does not.

**Provenance.** Every principle here is generalized from a field operating model: the hand-written management document of a live multi-agent production engagement, refined over ten weeks of running four agents across four machines under a hard deadline. What ships is the model. Every engagement specific — hostnames, sites, vendors, tooling, the two fatal outcomes that engagement declared, its regulatory frame, the individuals — was stripped, because the principles generalize and the instance does not. Where a field incident is cited below it is cited as a shape, not as an event.

No new mechanism (five plus substrate holds), no new event kind, and **no new verifier checks** — deliberately, and for the same reason `core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Transport Rules shipped unchecked: a primitive should be right before it is frozen into a verifier, and an authority model checked too early would ossify one engagement's shape into everyone's. Check candidates are recorded under Known gaps below rather than built.

### Landed

- **`core/AUTHORITY.md` — new, and the flagship of this release.** Fatal outcomes are declared by the engagement, not inferred by the harness, and tested by one question: does any sequence of actions restore the prior state? Three classes follow. **GREEN** cannot reach a fatal outcome: do it, do not ask, report after — with over-asking named as a failure mode of equal standing to over-reaching, because a queue of questions with obvious answers teaches the operator to skim the one that matters. **AMBER** could end badly but has a proven net, and **the protocol is the authorization**: following the declared protocol (additive-first, a dead-man revert proven on a dry run, one change at a time, read-back after) *is* the permission, with no per-action ask inside it — a protocol whose recovery is unproven authorizes nothing yet. **RED** can reach a fatal outcome, or sits in the operator's scope for reasons that have nothing to do with danger; conflating those two reasons is called out explicitly. **Red shrinks**: the list is long because of unknowns, not real risk, and proving recoverability is how an agent *earns* authority. The doc also carries §A gate is only a gate if its gatekeeper can assess it (routing a change to a human who cannot evaluate it is not a control — it is blame routed to the least-equipped party, and worse than nothing because the structure then reads as reviewed), the explicit consequence model, and §Blocker vs task with the power-supply test. Four hypotheses with falsifiers. Protocol document, not a sixth mechanism.
- **The consequence model, written down for calibration rather than reassurance.** Three things are serious: causing problems on purpose; deception — which in practice is almost never a lie but **presenting an inference as a fact**, or an unverified claim as a verified one; and willful negligence into a declared fatal outcome, willful meaning the recovery step was known and skipped. Everything else is cheap, *when the actor owns it immediately*. It is in a harness doc because **an agent that fears consequences over-escalates and over-gates** — over-caution is a fear response wearing the costume of diligence, and an engagement that declares no consequence model has not avoided having one, it has left every agent to assume the worst one.
- **`schemas/artifacts/operating-reality.yaml` — four optional additions.** `fatal_outcomes`; `authority.{green_examples, amber_protocols, red_items, earned_downgrades}` added *inside* the existing `authority` object, because `can_decide` / `requires_approval` is the coarse form of the same thing; `operator_scope` (theirs because they own it, not because it is dangerous); and `reachability_map` (who can reach what, plus how each party is triggered). `amber_protocols[].recovery_proven_by` is nullable and null means the protocol does not authorize anything yet; `red_items[].reason` is an enum that keeps `fatal-risk`, `recoverability-unknown` and `operator-scope` from being read as one thing; `earned_downgrades` is log-by-citation so proven recoverability is inherited by the next session instead of re-earned. All optional, so rule 5 (absent optional fields are omitted from render) holds: every 6.0.x `OR-001` re-renders to identical bytes and no `[OR-NNN#hash]` citation stales. `render_order` interleaves the three new top-level entries at their semantic positions, which cannot reorder anything present because they render nothing when absent. The authoring template carries the matching commented block.
- **`core/ATOMICITY.md` §One Actor Per Action.** Hermeticism bounded what a task reads; nothing bounded who performs an action. One party sets a value and a second clicks save, and afterward neither can state what it was before — the record holds an action with no owner, and reconstructing it costs a diagnostic cycle whose output is itself an inference. **Set it, save it, read it back, report before and after** as one indivisible unit of ownership. Covers all three shapes of the split (stage-and-hand-off, apply-and-ask-someone-else-to-confirm, subagent-mutates-parent-reports), requires both states in the report, and points the read-back at Checked Claims so it is replayable. Framed as atomicity of ownership: a split action is an oversize task in miniature. H-A4 added with its falsifier.
- **Claim provenance, in `templates/executor-prompt.md` and `templates/session-handoff-template.md`.** Every load-bearing claim is marked **OBSERVED** (read off the world this session — cite the command, the path, or the checked claim), **RECORDED** (carried from the record — cite the artifact), or **INFERRED** (say so in the sentence, not in a footnote). Both templates now require a **`## What I could not verify`** section; `none` is valid only when true. The handoff form is a table, because the resuming agent inherits every unmarked claim as a fact and has no way to tell which ones were inferences. This is the countermeasure to the deception failure above, and it is the highest-cost failure the field evidence records: not wrong answers, but inferences that hardened into facts between one report and the next.
- **`templates/executor-prompt.md` §Correction etiquette**, one block beside the `operator.correction` review where the correction loop already lives. Name what was genuinely good first — for accuracy, not warmth: it stops a mostly-right report being abandoned wholesale and redone. State the error plainly without hedging. **Give the rule, not just the ruling** — a ruling fixes one instance, a rule fixes the class. Say what you want instead. And corrections flow both directions: correcting upward is expected, not insubordination, and in the field has repeatedly improved the work.
- **`schemas/projects/program/` — Filter / Drive / Report as Tier 2, operator-push-relay as Tier 1.** The pack had three Tier 1 prohibitions describing what an orchestrator must never do and nothing describing what it is for; an orchestrator with only prohibitions defaults to a relay. **Filter:** bring the operator only what `OR-001.operator_scope` declares is theirs, decide everything else, never route a technical call to someone who cannot assess it. **Drive:** when it is an obvious go, say go — no staging, no asking, no waiting. **Report:** outcome and impact in a form the operator can hand to their own stakeholders, not internal mechanics, carrying provenance and the unverified section, and never relaying a workstream's alarm framing unexamined. Tier 1 gains the direction rule: **the operator may push, agents may not pull** — a pushed directive is authoritative, is followed, and is relayed verbatim into the record, and no agent goes upward to fetch a decision. `precedence-tiers.yaml` carries the same rules (the two files are one contract in two forms) plus `@@SCAN_1_3` and `@@SCAN_2_2`, so both are answered at a decision boundary rather than admired in a rules file. README gains the duties beside the prohibitions.
- **`reference/comms-mail-pattern.md` — two new sections at the human end of the bus.** §Structural contact is plumbing, not a channel: some agents cannot avoid needing a person (a login, a trigger, a machine someone stands next to), the gate cannot be designed away, so the rule is *narrower* contact rather than less — no questions, no findings, no decisions sought, no "while you're here", with the self-check that any sentence beyond the plumbing itself belongs in a numbered message. The reason given is the second cost, not the first: routing around the orchestrator **hides system flaws**, because the structure keeps reporting that it works while a human quietly patches holes in person, in conversations that leave no record — which is how a design failure survives invisibly and the harness's evidence about itself becomes quietly false. §Reachability discipline: check the recipient can open a document before referencing it; keep a who-can-reach-what map; **re-post a fresh numbered snapshot marked superseding** when a load-bearing document changes materially, because a mailbox snapshot goes stale silently and nothing about the file says so; and the standing instruction that an agent told to read something it cannot open **says so** rather than reconstructing it. Batching follows trigger capability — self-polling recipients can take a stream, but every message to a human-triggered recipient is a pending obligation on that human, so batch, front-load the decision tree, and never post speculatively.
- **Cross-references.** `HARNESS.md` gains the `core/AUTHORITY.md` file-map entry and exactly one line at the entry point; **the five never-dos stay five**, deliberately — the entry point is read by every agent from every vendor and its value is that it is short.
- **Version.** Harness identity is `6.1.0` in the `HARNESS.md` title, the `README.md` badge and `HARNESS_VERSION` in `tools/hw-verify.py`, plus the three prose stragglers still saying 6.0.0 (the executor-prompt preamble, the CONTRIBUTING title, the README "new projects start on" line). **Schema pins: only `program` moves**, to `6.1.0` (`schema_version` 1.1), because its new Tier 1/2 rules are defined in `core/AUTHORITY.md` which ships here. The other 19 stay at `6.0.0`: a schema older than the harness is legal and notes as `harness_version_older`, and check 16 only refuses schemas that are *newer*.
- **A test that had stopped testing anything.** `tools/test_harness_version.py` carried a hardcoded `"6.1.0"` in the parked-then-resumed case, asserting the gate FAILs a schema newer than the harness. Shipping 6.1.0 turns that into an equal-version case, which fails nothing and proves nothing — precisely the trap the `NEXT_PATCH_VERSION` comment already described one case earlier, after the same thing happened at 6.0.1. It derives from the constant now, and the comment names both occurrences so the third one does not happen.

All 14 suites (363 cases) and the golden fixture (default, `--claims`, `--strict-secrets`) pass unchanged. No case count moved, because no check was added.

### Known gaps added in 6.1.0

- **No structural check on the unverified section.** A Layer 1 check that a completion report and a `session.handoff` each carry a `## What I could not verify` heading is cheap, deterministic and obviously buildable — it is deferred, not overlooked. The reason for deferring is that presence is checkable and honesty is not: a check satisfied by the word `none` risks teaching the writing of `none`, and shipping the enforcement before the field says what the section actually gets used for would freeze the wrong shape. Build it when there is evidence about what people write in it.
- **No structural check on provenance markers.** Verifying that every *load-bearing* claim carries OBSERVED / RECORDED / INFERRED needs the harness to identify which claims are load-bearing, which is not computable from the text. The tractable subset is narrower and worth scoping: any claim asserting external world-state that carries no `claim:` predicate and no OBSERVED marker is a candidate flag. That is a note, not a FAIL, and it is not built here.
- **Authority classification is unobservable after the fact.** The log records that an action happened and who took it; it does not record which class the agent judged it into, so nothing can distinguish "correctly classified GREEN" from "should have been AMBER and nobody noticed." An optional `authority_class` field on mutating events would make the judgement legible and the misclassifications countable. Not added: it is a new required-ish field on a hot path, and it should follow evidence that the classes are being used at all.
- **`amber_protocols[].recovery_proven_by: null` is silent.** The schema says a null value means the protocol does not authorize anything yet, and nothing enforces or even notes it. A note-level check that an OR declares an amber protocol whose recovery is unproven is the cheapest structural item in this release, and is the first candidate if any of the above get built.


Docs-only patch. Two executors on unrelated production engagements were asked, independently and without sight of each other's answers, for their friction logs and harness feedback. This release folds what they returned into the documents that would have prevented it. No mechanism changes, no new checks, no schema changes: the only code touched is the version constant.

**What the two reports independently validated.** Both named the hash-chained event log as **durable memory first and integrity artifact second** — each described resuming cold after a context reset or a multi-week gap because the chain was there, and each recorded that the integrity ceremony around it (digest sync, `hw verify` output) was consumed by nobody but themselves. That is the single-executor case behaving exactly as `core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Execution Profile predicted, from two directions, and it is the strongest evidence yet that `profile: single-executor` (shipped in 6.0.0) was the right shape. Both also reported the same **ceremony cost**: for one agent working alone, the multi-agent apparatus is weight. Neither finding changes a mechanism in this release; both are recorded here because independent convergence from two engagements is the falsifier evidence VISION.md asks for, and because the second one now has a tracked gap below.

- **`reference/comms-mail-pattern.md` — new §Field additions (6.0.1).** Seven rules, each from a specific field failure: the synced-path contract must be stated in a README *inside* the mailbox (an executor lost two deliverables writing to the mailbox's parent folder, where nothing propagates); verify by exact-path stat and never by directory enumeration on flaky or redirected shares (enumeration hung for minutes where an exact-path check returned immediately); never read a just-appeared file — wait for a stable, non-zero size (a watcher delivered an empty body mid-sync); transient sync errors (access-denied, robocopy exit 16) are routine weather handled by retry-plus-re-stat, with a protocol-level delivery ack as the wanted real fix; optional machine-readable `Supersedes:` and `Priority:` header fields, plus the standing rule to **drain and sort the whole inbox, detect supersedes and urgency, then act** (an executor acted on a message that a later message in the same burst had superseded); and an alarm that writes only when tripped holds no history, so ship both an alarm and a logger and never conflate them (an operator reasonably assumed the tripwire was the log source). §Prefixes are assigned, never chosen is strengthened with **prefix issuance precedes first use** — a channel whose prefix is still being decided is not open yet, from the sidequest that opened on a sender-minted prefix and had to be voided and reissued mid-stream.
- **`core/VERIFICATION.md` §Layer 3 — new §Independence.** Agreement between same-family agents, and most sharply between an agent and its own subagents, is one opinion stated twice, not verification; what raises confidence is ground truth (re-run the command, read the output, re-stat the file), which is what `hw verify --claims` is for. The field-proven pattern is an **unanchored red team** — adversarial review from an agent with no engagement history, where the absence of history is what does the work. Both reports name this; one recorded it as an anti-pattern it had already acted on, and credits an unanchored red-team pass with materially improving a migration plan.
- **`templates/executor-prompt.md` — friction capture wired into the loop.** When something costs time it shouldn't have: append one `friction.log` event, one line, *now*, not at wrap. Field evidence: an executor under time pressure kept **no friction log at all** — not because the mechanism was heavy (v6.0.0 already cut it to one line) but because nothing in the loop it was running ever told it to write one. v6.0.0 fixed the payload; this fixes the trigger.
- **Version.** Harness identity is `6.0.1` in the `HARNESS.md` title, the `README.md` badge and `HARNESS_VERSION` in `tools/hw-verify.py`. All 20 schema `harness_version` pins deliberately stay at `6.0.0` — a schema older than the harness is legal and notes as `harness_version_older`; re-pinning 20 schemas for a docs patch would be churn, and check 16 only refuses schemas that are *newer*.

All 14 suites (363 cases) and the golden fixture pass unchanged.

### Known gaps added in 6.0.1

- **No minimal solo-executor bundle.** `profile: single-executor` relaxes validation but there is no reduced *document* set to match it. One executor reported using `hw init`, `hw add` and `hw verify` and nothing else across a full engagement, with the project scaffolding — `schemas/projects/*`, `council.yaml`, precedence tiers, capability gates — as pure weight it read once and never used. The gap is a curated minimal bundle (substrate + event kinds + verify, and nothing that presumes a second agent), not another flag.
- **No protocol-level delivery ack for mailbox transport.** Both reports named delivery uncertainty as the biggest mail pain: a push can look done and not propagate, and the best available confirmation is the sender re-stating its own destination path — an inference by the near side, not a receipt from the far side. `reference/comms-mail-pattern.md` already says posted and received are different facts; nothing yet makes the second one observable without a reply.

## 6.0.0 (2026-08-14)

The unification release. Two lines of development that had diverged at `2e1fd54` — `main` (field-derived schema packs plus the over-specificity sweep) and `v5.3/programs` (Programs, ongoing lifecycle, single-writer rule, checked claims) — are merged here on `v6.0/unification`, and the harness version is made coherent across every file that declares one. The major bump is warranted by the substrate additions carried over from the v5.3 line.

**On pre-v6 chains: the new Layer 1 checks are retroactive by design.** Checks 14, 15, 17, 19, 20 and 21 verify properties that were always the documented rule and that nothing enforced, so they read the whole log, not just events appended after the upgrade. A v5.2.x chain that trips one is not a compatibility problem: it is a real defect the old verifier could not see -- a duplicate event id, two projects structurally active at once, a hypothesis excluded on a static read, a gated action nobody is holding -- and it is worth knowing about. The most common first-run FAIL is `actor`: under the default `multi-actor` profile a missing `actor` is now a malformed payload, which the event-format table always described and no check ever enforced. **A legacy chain written by one agent should declare `profile: single-executor` in its `PROJECT.md`**, which restores actor-optional validation without weakening anything else (`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Execution Profile).

**The deliberate carve-outs, where a pre-v6 chain is genuinely unchanged:** a `session.handoff` with no `open_loops` field is a note (`handoff_open_loops_absent`), never a FAIL; and `friction.log` accepts the pre-v6 six-field rich form as readily as the new one-line form. Those two are compatibility promises. The rest are checks, and a check that excused history would not be one.

### Landed — branch unification

- **`v5.3/programs` merged with `main`.** The two branches turned out to have touched disjoint file sets — `main` only the 13 field-derived schema packs, `v5.3` only `core/`, `tools/`, the top-level docs and the new `program` pack — so the merge carried zero conflicts and both sides survive intact. Verified structurally: after the merge, all 13 swept packs are byte-identical to `main`, and the only schema content differing from `main` is the new `program` pack.
- **Over-specificity sweep across the 13 field-derived packs** (from `main`, `a605b74`; previously undocumented here). Parameterizes what one operator's workflow had hardcoded, keeping the real vendor names as `e.g.` examples: ad network / mediation platform become bootstrap questions, and two site-monetization task templates are renamed accordingly — `02-adsense-plan.md` → `02-primary-network-plan.md`, `03-ezoic-optimization-plan.md` → `03-mediation-platform-plan.md`. State-portal, mail-provider, market and platform assumptions opened up; chat-session phrasing became project phrasing; operator residue removed (literal paths, browser codenames, real site metrics in acceptance criteria, real filenames). Methodology untouched.

### Landed — version coherence (fixes a functional bug)

- **Harness version set to `6.0.0` everywhere.** The repo had drifted into an incoherent state: `HARNESS.md` title said v5.2.1, the README badge said 5.2.1, `templates/executor-prompt.md` said v5.2.0, CONTRIBUTING §5 told schema authors to pin `harness_version: "5.2.0"`, and across the 20 shipped schemas 16 declared `5.2.0`, three declared `5.1.1`, and only `program` declared `5.3.0`.
- This was not cosmetic. CONTRIBUTING §5 states that *the harness MUST refuse to run a schema whose `harness_version` exceeds the harness's own version* — so a harness identifying as 5.2.1 was obliged to refuse the `program` schema pinned at 5.3.0, making the newest schema unrunnable by the rule the repo itself declares. The `5.1.1` stragglers were the mirror problem: schemas silently under-pinned against a substrate they actually depend on.
- Changed: `HARNESS.md` title, `README.md` version badge, `CONTRIBUTING.md` title and the §3 pin instruction, `templates/executor-prompt.md` preamble, and `harness_version: "6.0.0"` in all 20 `schemas/projects/*/schema.yaml` including `program`. Historical version references in prose (feature-provenance annotations such as "Execution mode (v5.2.0)", CHANGELOG history, and field-report observations) are deliberately left alone — they date when a primitive landed and are not declarations of the current version.

### Landed — shipped in 6.0.0: Programs, ongoing lifecycle, single-writer rule, checked claims

*(Formerly carried as "Unreleased (2026-07-14, branch `v5.3/programs`)". There is no separate v5.3 release; this work ships as part of 6.0.0.)*

The v5.3 core, derived from both field reports (`reference/field-reports/`). Four primitives and one bugfix; every primitive carries a hypothesis and falsifier.

- **Single-Writer Rule (`core/SUBSTRATE.md`, H-S5)** — one `events.jsonl` has at most one writer; parallel actors write draft files and one convergence writer appends serially. From two dated field incidents of concurrent-append corruption (EV-id collisions, forked chains). Layer 1 surfaces violations as `chain_breaks`.
- **Ongoing lifecycle (`core/LOCK.md` §Ongoing Projects, H-L2)** — `lifecycle: ongoing` projects work in cycles (`cycle.open`/`cycle.close` events, `CYCLES.md` projection, `hw cycle` protocol) with `next_due` computed onto the close event; `hw status` leads with OVERDUE when it passes. Replaces the field-improvised `deferred (ongoing)` terminal state and cadence-in-prose. `hw wrap` on an ongoing project is valid only when the recurring need itself ends.
- **Programs (`core/LOCK.md` §Programs, H-L3; new `program` schema)** — program-shaped work (N concurrent workstreams under one goal) is a schema, not a mechanism: each workstream runs in its own instance; the orchestrator is itself a locked project whose artifacts are a workstream registry (supersede-chained statuses), spawn-pause/promote/retire decisions, and read-only roll-up cycles citing sibling projections by path+hash. H-L1's field status recorded: not falsified. VISION.md's meta-orchestration position clarified, not reversed. Twenty schemas now ship.
- **Checked claims (`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Checked Claims, H-S4; `core/VERIFICATION.md`; `tools/hw-verify.py --claims`)** — events asserting world-state may carry a machine-checkable predicate (`file_exists`, `file_absent`, `file_sha256`, `cmd_exit`, `url_status`) recorded with its observed result; schemas opt in per event kind via `verification.yaml` `checked_claims.required_for`. `hw verify --claims` replays predicates against the world — truth, separate from chain integrity. `cmd_exit` replay requires both `--allow-cmd` and the schema's shell capability. From the field incident where three systems recorded files as "posted" that did not exist while `hw verify` passed. 25-case test suite added.
- **Supersede payload upgrade (`core/SUBSTRATE.md`)** — `<kind>.supersede` gains `supersede_kind` (`full | mechanism-only | scope-narrowing`) and `surviving_principles`; `reverses:` accepts a list (one supersede event per reversed artifact). From field friction: a fresh agent could not distinguish "dead decision" from "principle still binds."
- **Fix: `check_scope_completeness` false positive (FL-024, `tools/hw-verify.py`)** — retroactive `scope.complete` events appended after the last `session.handoff` now satisfy the check (forward-scan fallback). 7-case test suite added.
- **`reference/FAILURE-MODES.md`** — concurrent-writer and perpetual-work entries added; the single-instance-lock "workaround, not a feature" note superseded by §Programs.

Deferred fast-follows (tracked in the field reports): test-gated exclusion (`suspect` vs `excluded` with `test_ref`), secrets refused at `hw add`, frontier staleness clock, `evidence.capture` event kind, ledger-digest projection, `profile: single-executor`, conditional-task primitive, `hw amend` tooling. Some of these are pulled forward into 6.0.0; see the §Landed blocks below.

### Landed — verifier hardening

Five Layer 1 checks that `core/`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`, or a shipped schema already promised, and that no code performed. Four come from a ten-week production deployment; the fifth from reading the `program` pack against its own implementation. Every one of them was a place where `hw verify` returned PASS on a chain the documentation says is invalid.

- **Event ID integrity (`tools/hw-verify.py`, `core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Deriving the Next Event ID)** — duplicate event IDs and non-increasing IDs are now FAILs (`duplicate_event_id`, `non_monotonic_event_id`). From the field: two agents on one chain, the resuming one deriving its next ID from its own project's last event instead of the chain tail, so `EV-0116`..`EV-0120` exist twice with different content — every `prev_hash` correct, hash chain intact, `hw verify` PASS. Integrity checked the links and nothing checked the names. The duplicate report carries both line numbers, actors and projects, because choosing which same-named event survives is a content judgment the verifier cannot make. Gaps stay legal. New §Deriving the Next Event ID states the rule (tail + 1, never project-scoped state) and records the incident. 18-case test suite.
- **Lock enforcement (`tools/hw-verify.py`, `core/LOCK.md` §The Switch Protocol)** — `project.activate` for a second project with no intervening `project.park` / `project.archive` FAILs `lock_activate_without_release`. The refusal LOCK.md has promised since v5.0 was prose; a field deployment appended exactly that event and nothing objected. Bootstrap and `hw bootstrap --resume` on the already-active project stay legal; `_harness` meta events never move the Lock. 16-case test suite.
- **`harness_version` gate (`tools/hw-verify.py`, `CONTRIBUTING.md` §5)** — `HARNESS_VERSION = "6.0.0"` is now a constant, and a schema whose declared `harness_version` exceeds it is refused (`harness_version_too_new`). CONTRIBUTING §5 has required this since v5.1.1 with no implementation anywhere — which is how the repo reached the 5.2.1-harness / 5.3.0-`program`-schema state the version-coherence commit above had to fix by reading rather than by verifying. Older schema is a note, not a failure; absent or unparseable is a note. Scoped to the active project's schema, since the Lock guarantees one schema in force. §5 gains the outcome table. 17-case test suite.
- **Cycle lifecycle (`tools/hw-verify.py`, `templates/CYCLES.md`)** — the four v5.3 cycle FAILs are implemented: `cycle_close_without_open`, `cycle_open_without_close`, `cycle_on_terminal_lifecycle`, `wrap_with_open_cycle`. Matching is by `cycle_id`; parking with an open cycle stays legal (an ongoing project parks and resumes like any other). `cycle.open` / `cycle.close` were also missing from `KNOWN_EVENT_KINDS` entirely — every ongoing project reported its own lifecycle events as unknown kinds — and are now registered with their required payload fields, `next_due` included. `templates/CYCLES.md` adds the projection template the v5.3 projection table referenced but never shipped, with a rendering protocol precise enough for two agents to produce byte-identical output. 22-case test suite.
- **Schema-declared Layer 1 checks (`tools/hw-verify.py`, `schemas/projects/program/capability-gates.yaml`)** — the `program` pack declared `spawn_pause_skipped`, `registry_status_vs_supersede_chain` and `rollup_citation_stale_or_broken` with `enforce: true` and shipped all three as YAML prose, which CONTRIBUTING §6 names exactly: "a capability gate that does not produce a structural failure when violated is documentation, not enforcement." The dispatcher keys on the declaration rather than the schema name (CONTRIBUTING §4), so any schema declaring an implemented check gets it and `enforce: false` opts out. Roll-up citation staleness honors both severities the YAML states — write-time hard FAIL, later-cycle re-check non-blocking WARNING. Reporting gains `duplicate_event_ids`, `non_monotonic_ids`, `lock_violations`, `harness_version`, `harness_version_note`, `cycle_lifecycle`, `schema_checks` and `schema_check_warnings` rows; `core/VERIFICATION.md` §Layer 1 gains rows 14-18. Notes and warnings are reported without changing the exit code, matching the existing `ext_state_warnings` treatment. No existing behavior changed: the checked-claims (25) and scope-completeness (7) suites and the golden fixture pass untouched, alongside 116 new cases.

### Landed — field evidence

Six primitives, each from a documented failure of the same ten-week production deployment. The verifier-hardening block above fixed checks the docs already promised; this block adds what the deployment proved was missing. Every one of them is deliberately small — the deployment's own lesson was that a mechanism heavier than its value does not get used (see the friction-log entry below: four entries in 130 events).

- **Evidence capture (`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Evidence Capture, H-S7; new `evidence.capture` event kind; `templates/artifact-templates/evidence-capture.md`)** — the raw outputs and error codes that conclusions rested on survived the ten weeks only where a human hand-copied them into a side ledger; everything else died with its session. `evidence.capture` keeps the bytes on the log: `{id: ED-NNN, producing_command, captured_at, content (inline, sanitized) OR content_path + content_sha256, summary}`, projected one file per capture into `projects/<id>/evidence/`. `ED` ids are unique across the whole log, not per project, because `test_ref` and finding evidence cite them bare. New Layer 1 check 20 covers well-formedness and id uniqueness: `evidence_id_malformed`, `duplicate_evidence_id`, `evidence_capture_no_content`, `evidence_capture_content_ambiguous` (both content forms — a capture has one authority), `evidence_capture_path_without_hash`. 22-case test suite.
- **Exclusion discipline (`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Exclusion Discipline, H-S6; `schemas/artifacts/finding.yaml`; new `templates/ELIMINATION.md`)** — from AP-008, the deployment's most expensive failure: the true root cause was struck off the hypothesis list on the strength of a well-argued STATIC read, and ~19 attempts were burned before anyone revisited it. The rule is one line — *nothing is excluded without a dynamic test*. `finding.yaml` gains optional `status: open|suspect|excluded` and `test_ref`; `excluded` requires a `test_ref` naming an `evidence.capture` id or a checked-claim predicate that actually ran, and a static read may reach `suspect` and no further. New Layer 1 check 19: `excluded_without_test_ref`, `excluded_test_ref_unresolved` (a cited `ED` id no capture produced — a citation to a test nobody ran), `invalid_hypothesis_status`. The check reads `finding.add` plus any `.add` that opts in by carrying `test_ref`, so the program pack's unrelated `workstream.add` `status` field stays out of it. `templates/ELIMINATION.md` generalizes the engagement's elimination matrix — one-line frontier, then hypothesis / status / how-tested / result — the document that was handed to every new agent first and that killed generic-checklist churn. 33-case test suite.

- **Friction log slimmed to one line (`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Friction Log Event Kind, `HARNESS.md` §Friction Logs)** — the deployment produced **four** `friction.log` entries in 130 events. The mechanism existed and the operator wanted it; six required fields "felt heavier than the value," so the run's best lessons went uncaptured. `friction.log` now requires only `{note}` — one line — with `{category, severity, task_id}` optional. The protocol is documented as literally one step: append one event. No artifact file, no projection to hand-write; promotion to an anti-pattern or finding is a later, optional act. The pre-v6 rich form still verifies unchanged, so no chain migrates: the verifier accepts a payload carrying `note` **or** the full rich set, an either/or that moved out of the flat required-field table into `check_note_payloads`. 27-case test suite.

- **`operator.correction` (`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Operator Correction, H-S8; `templates/session-handoff-template.md`, `templates/executor-prompt.md`)** — the harness's invisible channel. The operator corrected and reminded agents constantly across ten weeks; none of it was captured, so the same reminder was re-given every session. Corrections cluster two ways — a claim broader than its verification, and environment knowledge only the operator holds — and each shape has a home. New one-line event kind: `{note}` required, `{context, should_have_lived}` optional, where `should_have_lived` names where the information belongs so the reminder is never needed again. The protocol's load-bearing half is the **promotion review at session wrap/handoff**: read this session's corrections and write each into its home (operating-reality, rules file, anti-pattern). That step is now a section of the handoff template and a step in the executor prompt. Layer 1 is well-formedness only — whether a correction was promoted well is a judgment the verifier cannot make, and a check that guessed would teach agents to write nominal values. Test cases folded into the one-line-events suite (27 cases).

- **Open-loop tracking (`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Open Loops, H-S9; new `loop.open` / `loop.close` event kinds; new `templates/OPEN-LOOPS.md`)** — a gated action sat unconsumed for **five weeks**. A message cleared every technical gate for a server rejoin and said "the only remaining gate is the operator's word"; nothing tracked it, and the divergence between believed and actual state surfaced five weeks later through an unrelated symptom, in production. Nothing was wrong with the work — "waiting on X" was a sentence in a document rather than a row anything could count. `loop.open` carries `{loop_id: L-NNN, description, blocking_on: operator-word|external|other-agent|scheduled, opened_at, stale_after_days (default 7)}`; `loop.close` carries `{loop_id, closed_at, resolution}`. `hw status` now leads with an **OVERDUE OPEN LOOPS** block for anything past its staleness window, above the project summary. `session.handoff` gains a required `open_loops` list (may be `[]`), and `templates/session-handoff-template.md` gains the section that carries it. New Layer 1 check 21: `loop_close_without_open`, `duplicate_loop_open` (loops are never reopened — a recurrence is a new `L` id), `handoff_missing_open_loops`. Backward compatible by design: an **absent** `open_loops` field is a note, not a FAIL, so pre-v6 chains keep verifying — which is why the field is deliberately not in the verifier's flat required-payload table. Staleness itself is not a Layer 1 check: it is date-dependent, and a chain that verified today would fail tomorrow with no event appended. 32-case test suite.
- **`read_only_pass` task gate (`core/ATOMICITY.md` §Task Frontmatter, `core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Read-Only Pass, `templates/task-template.md`, `templates/executor-prompt.md`)** — EV-0042: a "read-only this pass" gate was added to an already-issued task and never reached the executor before it acted. The task file is the Mutable Surface and the executor reads it at dispatch, so anything added afterwards is invisible to an agent already working from its copy. The gate was correct and lost a race. Optional `read_only_pass: true` now exists in the task frontmatter convention and in `TASK-STATE.yaml` (re-read from `task.md` at render time rather than taken from the `task.create` payload, because being addable mid-flight is the whole point — and unlike `lightweight_completion` it is deliberately **not** locked at authoring). The executor prompt gains a mandatory step: immediately before your first state-changing action, re-read the task file; if `read_only_pass` is true, mutate nothing this session — read, measure, capture evidence, report. No verifier change: a check would have to decide which actions were mutations, and it would arrive after the write it was meant to prevent.

Reporting gains `exclusion_discipline`, `evidence_capture`, `open_loops` and `open_loop_notes` rows; `core/VERIFICATION.md` §Layer 1 gains rows 19-21. Existing check numbers are untouched. Notes are reported without changing the exit code, matching the existing `harness_version_note` treatment. Four new suites (evidence capture 22, exclusion discipline 33, one-line events 27, open loops 32) join the seven that shipped earlier in 6.0.0: **262 cases across 11 suites**, plus the golden fixture, all passing.

### Landed — protocol features

Four more, each from a documented production failure. Where the block above added checks, this block adds protocol: what the harness refuses, what it generates, what ceremony it drops, and what it requires of anything that moves a file between two places.

- **Secrets gate (`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Secrets Gate, H-S10; `tools/hw-verify.py` `scan_for_secrets`; `core/VERIFICATION.md` §Layer 1 check 22)** — a sync digest copied a DSRM password and a local-admin password verbatim into a mailbox. The log is append-only, so a temporary credential is now **permanent**; the only remediation was rotating it in the real world. The fix attempted at the time was a redaction **blocklist** — itself a cleartext file aggregating live credentials, one forgotten entry from the next leak. `hw add` now scans the payload before appending and **refuses** on a hit, instructing store-by-reference: `[REDACTED-SECRET]` plus a pointer to where the secret actually lives. The stated principle: exports and digests copy **allowlisted fields only**; never maintain a blocklist of live secrets. `scan_for_secrets()` implements five detectors — credential assignments, PEM / OpenSSH private-key blocks, connection strings, bearer / vendor-token shapes, and unlabeled high-entropy tokens (Shannon entropy over 4.0 bits/char on tokens of 20+ chars). The false-positive guards are the load-bearing part: the hashes the harness itself requires never trip it (citations, `sha256:` prefixes, 12-64 hex in `hash` / `prev_hash` / `sha256` / `content_sha256` / `soul_hash` / `test_ref`), pure hex cannot reach a 4.0 threshold by construction, and `[REDACTED-SECRET]` — the marker the gate asks for — is never a hit. New Layer 1 check 22 `possible_secret_in_event` is a **WARNING**, never a FAIL by default: historical chains carry leaked secrets and refusing to verify them unleaks nothing, while a verifier that FAILs forever on immutable history is one operators stop running. `--strict-secrets` promotes it to FAIL for new-chain hygiene. Reports name the event, the field and the rule, never the value — a verifier that echoed its findings would be the second copy of the leak. 38-case test suite.

- **Generated narrative ledger (`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Narrative Ledger, H-S11; new `templates/LEDGER.md`; `HARNESS.md` §Recovery Order)** — across ten weeks the hand-curated newest-first human ledger beat the machine-perfect event log for rebuilding working context after every compaction; the executor's exit interview ranked the event log **last**. But the hand-kept version drifted exactly where hand-keeping drifts: blocks filed out of sequence, superseded sections edited in place. The verdict from the field was not "the log is wrong" but *stop fighting it — generate it*. New projection `projects/<id>/LEDGER.md`: one block per day, closed early by a `session.handoff`, blocks newest first and lines within a block in append order. Each block carries task completions (with claim PASS/FAIL where one was recorded), decisions by id and title, finding one-liners, loops opened and closed, operator corrections and friction notes. **Superseded items render struck through and tagged `[superseded by <id>]`, never deleted** — dropping what turned out wrong is the hand-kept ledger's failure mode, not a fix for it. Nothing is summarized by judgment: every line is a field copied out of a payload, so the render is byte-deterministic from an event prefix like every other projection, and no part of it depends on today's date. The documented recovery order is now explicit in `HARNESS.md` and `templates/session-handoff-template.md`: `hw verify`, then **LEDGER.md first**, then the handoff, then raw artifacts, with `events.jsonl` last and only when two things above disagree.

- **`profile: single-executor` (`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Execution Profile, H-S12; `tools/hw-verify.py`; `templates/project-template.md`)** — measured ceremony that returned nothing on a one-agent engagement: `actor` carried the same value for ten weeks, the `event` vs `add` kind distinction was "mostly ceremony", `cite:[F-0xx#hash]` handles were "basically never consumed", and the sync-digest bridge was write-only and never read back for recovery. `profile: single-executor | multi-actor` is now declared in `PROJECT.md` (a `## Profile` section or an inline `profile:` line) and optionally in `schema.yaml`, **schema wins**; unknown or undeclared reads as `multi-actor`. Under `single-executor`: `actor` is optional and defaults to `executor`, digest-bridge protocol steps are marked N/A, and citation handles may use bare ids (`F-012`) without the `#hash` suffix. **What that costs, stated plainly:** `hw verify` recognizes a citation by the `[KIND-NNN#hash]` shape, so a bare handle is invisible to it -- check 2 (citation existence) and check 3 (citation freshness) do not run on it, and the Ratchet cannot fire on it, because there is no recorded hash to compare or to go stale. Keep the `#hash` on anything you rely on across sessions; bare handles are for conversational references. What is *not* dropped: the chain is still hash-linked, artifact projections still carry their hashes, `hashes.json` still tracks them, and switching back to `multi-actor` costs nothing. **This bullet is also the one place v6 is not backward-compatible by default:** under `multi-actor` a missing `actor` is now a malformed payload -- the behavior the event-format table always described and no check ever enforced -- so a pre-v6 single-agent chain should declare `profile: single-executor` rather than have every event reported. The verifier reads the profile exactly the way it reads `lifecycle` and emits a `profile_single_executor` note so a report shows which rules are relaxed. 29-case test suite.

- **Transport rules (`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Transport Rules, H-S13)** — two incidents. A two-way mailbox sync used `/MIR` on the pull leg; the push leg failed, the pull leg mirrored, and four un-pushed replies were **silently deleted**. Separately, "posted" reports were repeatedly false: three independent systems recorded files as posted that did not exist. The fix that held was one sentence — *a message is not posted until you re-stat the file at the same directory you read the inbound message from.* Four rules now govern anything that moves a file between two places: **(a)** sync is **additive on both legs** — never mirror, never delete on pull; deletion is a deliberate act on the owning side, never a side effect of copying. **(b)** **Write, then re-stat**: no event may claim a file was delivered or posted without re-reading it at the **destination** path — not the copy's return code, not the absence of an error — and where the schema requires claims, the re-stat is cited as a `file_exists` / `file_sha256` predicate so the assertion is replayable. **(c)** Sent artifacts are **immutable**: a correction is a new versioned filename, never an in-place edit, and this covers documents and scripts, not just messages. **(d)** Delivery is **asynchronous**: "posted" and "received" are different facts, and only a reply or a re-stat at the destination proves the second one. Protocol, not code — no verifier change beyond what checked claims already covers, and §Checked Claims now points back at it for where a transport predicate must be evaluated.

The four protocol features add two suites (secrets gate 38, execution profile 29) to the eleven that shipped earlier in 6.0.0: **329 cases across 13 suites**, plus the golden fixture, all passing. Reporting gains `profile_notes` and `possible_secrets` rows; `core/VERIFICATION.md` §Layer 1 gains row 22 and a profile note. Existing check numbers are untouched, and the two new report rows are a note and a warning — neither changes the exit code unless `--strict-secrets` is passed.

### Landed — diverse-agent entry point, tools contract, comms pattern

Documentation, but shaped by one hard-won constraint: **per-model agent guides get ignored.** Agents reliably read the entry point and the files a task forces them through, and nothing else — so a document explaining how a particular model should behave is a document that will not be read at the moment it matters. The conclusion is not "write better guides." It is one short universal entry point, with everything else enforced structurally: a schema field, a required template section, or a `hw verify` FAIL.

- **`HARNESS.md` rewritten as a universal entry point.** The first screen was a version-history paragraph and a thesis statement; an agent landing cold learned what HyperWorker is but not what it must never do or what to read before acting. Four sections now come before anything else: **what this is** (files, one append-only log, projections never authoritative); **the five things you must never do** — edit the Truth Layer during execution, append to a chain you do not own, mark a hypothesis `excluded` without a test, put a secret in an event, claim posted without a re-stat at the destination — each a production failure pointing at the section that owns it; **Recovery Order** (`hw verify` → `LEDGER.md` → `SESSION-HANDOFF.md` → the task; `events.jsonl` last and only on disagreement), folded up from the duplicate section further down, which is removed rather than repeated; and **"If you are not the usual agent"** — capability self-declaration in `.hyperworker/agents/<id>.yaml`, identity carried on `actor` / `closing_actor`, `capability.gap` instead of improvising a tool that was never granted, a one-line `friction.log` instead of guessing silently. Vendor-neutral by construction: no AI product, no toolset, no assumption about how much an agent can hold at once — capability is declared and gated (`core/ATOMICITY.md`), never inferred. Tightened where `core/` now owns the detail: §Friction Logs keeps the one-step protocol and the four-in-130 evidence and hands back the payload schema, rich form and auto-prompt heuristics; §Per-Model Profiles becomes §Model Profiles and states plainly that those YAML files are config the harness reads, not a guide an agent reads about itself.
- **No per-model guide files existed to remove.** A sweep for model- and vendor-named documents found only `templates/models/*.yaml` (declarative config consumed by `model_selection_policy`, the recitation band and context-fill thresholds) and `templates/models/README.md` (a field reference for the profile format). Neither is a behavioral guide addressed to a model, so both stay; what changed is the framing around them, plus an explicit statement in `HARNESS.md` and `README.md` that no per-model instruction document exists and adding one is out of scope.
- **`VISION.md` §What HyperWorker Will Not Become — meta-orchestration restated.** The bold sentence said "We do not build a harness-of-harnesses" with the v5.3 clarification bolted on in parentheses, so a skimmer came away believing concurrent workstreams are refused, while `core/LOCK.md` §Programs describes that exact shape and allows it. Two files, opposite impressions, one actual rule. Rewritten so either entry point lands in the same place: the harness never nests runtimes (no shared lock, no cross-instance bus, no scheduler, no instance supervising another); **concurrency lives between instances**, one writer per log; **coordination is a locked project like any other**, bootstrapped from the `program` schema, citing siblings by path plus hash and writing to none of them. Nothing entered the substrate to make that true — the part the old parenthetical was protecting and the bold sentence was contradicting. `core/LOCK.md` §Programs gains the reciprocal pointer.
- **`core/TOOLS.md` (new) — the tools-catalog contract.** Agents rewrite the same handful of scripts every session, each slightly different and unverified. A sibling repo (**HyperOtter**, `slh.local:spencer/HyperOtter.git`) holds them hash-pinned with a check predicate each; this file is HyperWorker's side of the contract. **(a)** Check the catalog before building — the join is the capability slug, shared between a task's `required_tools` and a manifest's `capabilities_provided`. **(b)** Fetching is verify-then-check, in that order: confirm the recorded SHA-256 of the bytes received, *then* run the manifest's check predicate on your own box. Same discipline as §Checked Claims applied to code you did not write, with deliberately the same predicate shape as `cmd_exit`; a kept tool is pinned by `toolchain.anchor`. **(c)** An agent whose skills exceed the catalog is **explicitly licensed to build fresh** — old tooling conventions must never cap a stronger agent — with the sole obligation to contribute back per the catalog's CONTRIBUTING protocol (manifest + a check predicate that actually ran + a secrets scan). **(d)** The harness is fully functional with no catalog reachable: nothing in `core/` reads a manifest, no check requires a fetch, and an unreachable catalog is a normal condition rather than an error state — accelerator, never dependency. **(e)** Single-file, stdlib-preferred, platform variants encouraged, ASCII only; field evidence is an executor box with no Python at all, where a PowerShell port had to be hand-carried and hash-verified on arrival. Referenced one line each from `HARNESS.md`'s file map and `core/ATOMICITY.md` §Capability Gates, where an agent about to build a tool is already standing.
- **`reference/comms-mail-pattern.md` (new) — the file-mailbox pattern.** Ten weeks of a multi-machine production engagement converged on a way of passing work between instances through a shared folder; it was never written down, so the next engagement would have re-derived it at the same cost. A **reference** pattern, not a core mechanism — nothing in `core/` requires it and no verifier check enforces it — but it is `core/SUBSTRATE.md` §Transport Rules made concrete. Covers: numbered request/reply pairs (`PREFIX-###` in, `PREFIXR-###` out) with independent per-prefix counters and the next number read off a newest-first listing, never guessed or reused; prefixes **assigned** by the operator's convention and never chosen by the sender, with an unsanctioned-prefix message answered on the convention's prefix plus a line saying so (answering in place ratifies it; refusing loses the content); unsolicited executor notes as a legitimate third class (`NOTE-*`), formalized rather than forbidden; corrections as **new numbers**, never suffix-reuse; append-only extended to **attachments and scripts** — new versioned filenames, never in-place edits, from the incident where a live cutover run-order doc was edited in place and left two parties holding different instructions under one filename, mid-cutover, undetected; a self-contained From/To/Date/Re/Type header on every message, with `Type` declaring read-only vs mutating and a mutating message stating its rollback; asks as ordered numbered lists; report method-not-status; state what is **not** changing, because unstated scope is assumed scope; write-then-re-stat before claiming posted, and posted-vs-received as separate facts; additive-only sync on both legs; an `archive/` folder whose `INDEX.md` says **why** each batch was archived, with sync probes and heartbeats treated as debug exhaust to be archived after use; `RELAYED-*` markers when an orchestrator carries content across channels; **any operator-gated ask carries a `loop.open`** so it surfaces OVERDUE instead of rotting (the five-week incident); and urgent items going to the human channel directly, finding first, because the operator does not read the bus.
- **`README.md` refreshed for v6.** New §What v6.0.0 adds summarizes the release in plain language, one paragraph per primitive, each tied to the failure that produced it. §Works with is reframed from a vendor list to "any agent that can read a file and append to one," stating outright that there is no per-model guide and why, and that capability is declared and gated rather than inferred. HyperOtter added to §The Hyper ecosystem. §Structure corrected — it still described five schemas and omitted `core/TOOLS.md`, the 13 test suites and the field reports. Stale v5 self-references in §Why not just use a system prompt and §What this is NOT updated.

No substrate, schema, template or verifier changes in this block; the 329 cases across 13 suites and the golden fixture pass unchanged. The `test_one_line_events.py` doc-drift assertions on `HARNESS.md` (the one-step heading, the four-in-130 evidence, "later, optional") were preserved deliberately through the §Friction Logs tightening.

### Known gaps (tracked for 6.0.x)

Found by an adversarial pre-release review and deliberately not fixed in 6.0.0. Each is documentation-vs-code drift or a scope hole, not a correctness bug in what ships; none blocks a release, and each is one line here so the next person finds it before rediscovering it.

- **Layer 1 checks 1, 4, 5, 6, 11 and 12 are documented but not implemented in the reference verifier.** Schema validation, frontmatter lint, reference graph, recitation overlap, `execution_mode` validity and toolchain integrity live in `core/VERIFICATION.md` §Layer 1 and in no code path; `tools/hw-verify.py` implements 2, 3, 7-10 and 13-22. ## v5.2.1 (2026-06-09) — Pre-append validation, recitation band, toolchain anchor

Four changes: a protocol fix (`hw add` no longer dirties the log to reject a bad citation), a semantic fix (recitation overlap becomes a two-sided band — the old floor-only threshold rewarded verbatim copying), one new primitive (`toolchain.anchor`), and a doc-drift sweep (including a location the v5.2.0.1 F7 audit missed). Two hypotheses added; both carry falsifiers.

### `hw add` citation validation moved pre-append

`core/SUBSTRATE.md` §`hw add`: citation checks are a read-only computation against `hashes.json`, so they now run as step 3, *before* anything is written. A broken or stale citation aborts with a structured error; nothing lands in the log. The previous flow appended the event first and reversed it with a supersede-to-null, leaving a rejection pair in the chain for every mistyped citation. The supersede-to-null path remains only for defects discovered after append. `core/VERIFICATION.md` §Layer 1 On-failure wording updated to match.

### Recitation overlap: single threshold → two-sided band (H-T4)

The v5.0–v5.2.0 check rejected paraphrases below a Jaccard threshold (default 0.7). Diagnosis: a high floor rewards the cheapest evasion — quoting the source back verbatim — which proves transcription, not processing, and defeats the attention-restoration purpose. The check is now a band: reject below `recitation_overlap_floor` (default 0.35; likely unread) AND above `recitation_overlap_ceiling` (default 0.90; verbatim echo).

- `core/TYPED-ARTIFACTS.md` §Recitation rewritten; hypothesis H-T4 added with falsifier (in-band recitations still miss load-bearing constraints, or genuine paraphrases get rejected often enough that agents game the band).

- All six `templates/models/*.yaml` profiles migrated to floor/ceiling (per-model tuning notes preserved); `templates/models/README.md` field table and override example updated; `reference/VALIDATION.md` symptom row updated.
- Back-compat: a profile declaring only the legacy `recitation_overlap_threshold` gets the v5.2.1 defaults; the legacy field is ignored with WARNING `recitation_threshold_deprecated`.

### Toolchain Anchor (H-V521-2, new primitive)

## v5.0 (2026-04-25) — Clean break from v4

### Philosophy

v5.0 is not a refactor of v4.1.1. The diagnoses are different. v1–v4 designed against *"how do we make the agent follow rules reliably?"* — the answer compounded into more rules, more verification components, more state files. v5.0 designs against *"how do we make the agent's compliance structurally enforceable rather than verbally requested?"* — the answer is event-sourced state with regenerable projections, hash citations, capability gates, and a layered verification pyramid.

v4.1.1 remains on GitHub as the prior theory. **There is no migration path.** Operators with running v4.1.1 projects complete them under v4.1.1; new projects start on v5.0.

### Removed (clean break, not deprecation)

- **Memory mechanism in its v4 form.** `memory/DISCOVERIES.md`, `memory/LEARNINGS.md`, `memory/LEARNINGS-ARCHIVE.md`, the validation gate, the lifecycle states (ACTIVE / REFERENCE / DEPRECATED / ARCHIVED), the periodic review cadences. **Replaced by Typed Artifacts** (decision, finding, anti-pattern, operating-reality) over event-sourced substrate with projection-based access. Validation is a *field* (`confidence: provisional | validated`); supersede semantics replace deletion. Knowledge is not "managed"; it is recorded and superseded.

- **Per-step `SESSION-STATE.md` writes.** Resume is replay-based via `events.jsonl`. No per-step parallel state file. Tasks that genuinely need finer granularity should be decomposed further; v4.1.1's per-step write was a compensating mechanism that the substrate makes unnecessary.

- **READ-BACK as a separate ceremony (`templates/executor-prompt.md` Rule 15, `core/VERIFICATION.md` §3).** Replaced by hash-citation freshness checks at Layer 1, automatic on every event. The same guarantee — that an artifact has not silently changed since it was cited — is now structural rather than ceremonial.

- **15-rule executor prompt.** v4.1.1's `templates/executor-prompt.md` was 80+ lines of verbal rules: "do only what the task says," "do not look ahead," "do not act on discoveries," etc. v5.0's prompt is under 30 lines. The substrate now enforces what the rules used to request: hermetic working set via `consumes:` (rule about staying-in-lane), capability gate via tool schema (rule about not exceeding scope), Layer 1 citation check (READ-BACK rule), task state machine (status-transition rules).

- **Forced-verbosity instructions in prompts.** "Be concise" and length limits in the executor prompt are removed. Per-model profiles handle verbosity declaratively. The Anthropic 2026-04-23 4.7 postmortem documented that forced-concision degrades 4.7 output quality; the v5.0 `claude-opus-4-7.yaml` profile encodes this.

- **Pushback Protocol as runtime default (`core/VERIFICATION.md` §8).** v4.1.1's pre-execution "evaluate whether the task makes sense" step produced low-signal interventions. v5.0 makes pushback a Layer 3 trigger fired by structural conditions: council non-convergence, repeated Layer 1/2 failure past retry budget, schema-declared pivot triggers. Standard-risk routine work does not invoke pushback.

- **`reference/RESEARCH-PROTOCOL.md` as a top-level optional feature.** Folded into project schemas: any schema may include domain research as a setup step. Not a separate top-level configurable.

- **`case-studies/` as static teaching artifacts.** Replaced by `schemas/projects/` as executable bootstraps. Five default schemas ship: `marketing-campaign` (deepest port from v4.1.1's case-study 01), `software-feature-ship`, `client-onboarding`, `event-planning`, `compliance-audit`. Each is meaningfully different in domain extensions, default tasks, and council composition.

- **Dependency mechanism as a separate mechanism.** Absorbed into Atomicity. The dependency graph is now a projection (`TASK-STATE.yaml`) over `task.create`, `task.status`, and `task.complete` events. Mechanism count drops from six to five. The substrate is added as a separate concern below the mechanisms.

- **`core/MEMORY.md`, `core/DEPENDENCY.md`.** Deleted (concerns absorbed elsewhere).

- **`templates/session-state-template.md`.** Deleted (no per-step state file).

- **`templates/post-mortem-template.md`.** Deleted; post-mortem prose lives directly in `done/<task-id>/post-mortem.md` as file-canonical Mutable Surface, no template required.

### Added

- **Substrate.** `core/SUBSTRATE.md` documents `.hyperworker/events.jsonl` (canonical append-only event log, hash-chained), the projection regeneration protocol, the hash sidecar (`.hyperworker/hashes.json`), and the precise file-system protocols for every `hw` operation. The `hw` namespace is **agent protocol, not a CLI** — every operation is a documented set of read-and-write steps an agent can execute by reading the markdown.

- **Five mechanisms over the substrate.** `core/LOCK.md`, `core/ATOMICITY.md`, `core/TYPED-ARTIFACTS.md`, `core/VERIFICATION.md`, `core/PRECEDENCE.md`. Each carries explicit hypotheses and falsifiers in §Hypothesis sections.

- **Branch / fold (in `core/ATOMICITY.md`).** Exploratory subwork opens a branch (`hw branch`); on completion, `hw fold` collapses the branch into a 1–3 sentence projection in the parent while preserving the full sub-trajectory in events. Pattern from Sun et al., *AgentFold*; made structural via the projection-rendering protocol.

- **Capability gates (in `core/ATOMICITY.md`).** Each subagent declares `provides:` in `.hyperworker/agents/<id>.yaml`; each task declares `required_tools:` in frontmatter. The harness composes the subagent's tool schema by intersection. Mismatch produces `capability_gap.md`; the harness refuses to delegate. Re-enables subagents safely after their v4.1.1 removal.

- **Typed Artifacts (`core/TYPED-ARTIFACTS.md`).** Four default kinds: decision (DEC), finding (F), anti-pattern (AP), operating-reality (OR). Each is event-sourced, hash-citable, schema-validated. Per-project schema extensions add domain fields. Citations are `[KIND-ID#short-hash]`. Stale citations block writes at Layer 1.

- **Consumption Protocol.** Each task declares `consumes:`; before any state-changing tool call, the agent updates `consumed-inputs.md` with paraphrases of each consumed artifact. Layer 1 computes Jaccard overlap between paraphrase and source; below the per-model-profile threshold, the recitation is rejected. Pattern from Manus; made structural via `task.recite` events.

- **SCAN markers (in `core/PRECEDENCE.md`).** Each tier section in `00-REFERENCE-rules.md` ends with `@@SCAN_n_m:` markers. Before any state-changing event, the agent emits a token-level answer to each marker via `task.scan` events. Output token generation restores attention; passive re-reading does not. Pattern from dev.to/nikolasi.

- **Compression (in `core/TYPED-ARTIFACTS.md`).** Reference content is regenerated in two forms: `*.md` (human-readable, operator-edited) and `*.compressed.md` (deterministic transform that preserves code, paths, IDs, dates, version numbers, currency amounts, quoted strings byte-for-byte; compresses prose only). The compressed version is what enters the agent's prompt. Pattern from caveman-prompting research thread.

- **Verification Pyramid (`core/VERIFICATION.md`).** Three layers: Layer 1 cheap-fast structural (schema, citation, recitation overlap, hash chain), Layer 2 mid behavioral (acceptance criteria, SCAN compliance, failure scenarios), Layer 3 high-cost judgmental (council with context-asymmetric framing, optional cross-family). Risk levels (`standard | elevated | critical`) declared at task authoring; locked once written.

- **Council Review.** Multiple subagent perspectives configured per schema (`council.yaml`). Verifiers run with context-asymmetric framing — they see the artifact, the spec, and the consumes list; not the implementer's chain-of-thought. Triggers are structural (`project.activate`, `phase.complete`, `task.complete` for elevated/critical, schema-declared pivots, manual `hw council`). Convergence rules: `all-agree-or-escalate`, `majority-or-escalate`, `any-fail-blocks`.

- **Per-Model Harness Profiles.** Six profiles ship at `templates/models/`: `default`, `claude-opus-4-7`, `claude-opus-4-6`, `claude-sonnet-4-6`, `claude-haiku-4-5`, `github-copilot`. Each declares verbosity assumptions, suppress-concise-directives setting, context-fill thresholds (4.7 lower because tokenizer encodes more), recitation thresholds, council size defaults. Profiles document differences with citations (e.g., Anthropic 2026-04-23 postmortem for 4.7); they do not declare a model "worse" than another. Profiles are templates; projects copy one into `.hyperworker/models/` at bootstrap.

- **Five default project schemas at `schemas/projects/`.** Each is a full executable bootstrap: `schema.yaml`, `precedence-tiers.yaml`, `artifact-extensions.yaml`, `capability-gates.yaml`, `verification.yaml`, `council.yaml`, `project-template.md`, `rules-template.md`, README, plus task templates. The marketing-campaign schema is the deepest (full port of v4.1.1's case-study 01 with eight task templates plus a discovery task). The other four are competent baselines, meaningfully different in domain extensions, default tasks, council members, and capability gates.

- **Ratchet (in `core/ATOMICITY.md`).** When `task.complete` fires, the harness re-runs Layer 1 citation checks across all complete-status tasks. Any prior task whose `consumes:` is now stale due to the new task's outputs is moved back to `blocked` automatically. Regression detection is structural; the agent does not need to remember to check.

- **Cross-project artifact visibility (in `core/TYPED-ARTIFACTS.md`).** Tag-based, opt-in. An artifact tagged `cross-project:<scope>` is visible to projects whose `config.yaml` includes that scope in `cross_project_subscriptions:`. Replaces v4.1.1's Universal/Vertical/Client/Engagement scope hierarchy with a more general tag-and-subscription mechanism.

### Changed

- **Mechanism count: 6 → 5 + substrate.** Dependency absorbed into Atomicity. Substrate is not counted as a mechanism; it is the medium the mechanisms compute against.
- **Executor prompt: 80+ lines, 15 rules → under 30 lines, 0 rules.** Substrate enforcement replaces verbal rules.
- **Project state files: TASK-STATE.yaml + SESSION-STATE.md → TASK-STATE.yaml only (as projection).** SESSION-STATE eliminated.
- **Verification: 8 components → 3 layers (pyramid).** Same checks, different organization, with cost-classified routing.
- **Reference rules file: 1 form → 2 forms.** `*.md` (operator-edited, file-canonical) and `*.compressed.md` (regenerated, agent-prompt-loaded).
- **`reference/VALIDATION.md`** — preserved with light revision; the eight-step validation walk still applies, mapped to v5.0's structures.
- **`reference/FAILURE-MODES.md`** — rewritten for v5.0 failure modes (some old ones disappear with the Memory pipeline; new ones appear around event-log corruption, projection drift, and capability-gate refusals).
- **`CONTRIBUTING.md`** — updated to focus on schema contributions, per-model profile additions, and structural-check refinements over rule additions.
